Description activity 1 - Meet the Plant Cell
Plants make their own food through photosynthesis, a process that uses sunlight to produce sugars. Chloroplasts are the green organelles in plant cells that capture sunlight and turn it into chemical energy for the plant to grow and live.
Plant cells are called eukaryotic because they contain membrane-bound organelles, including a nucleus that stores genetic information. Many organelles have membranes that control what moves in and out, and plant cells also have a rigid cell wall outside the cell membrane that gives structure and support. Other important organelles include mitochondria for releasing energy and vacuoles for storing water and nutrients.

Description activity 2 - Unicellular Organisms

Description
Unicellular organisms, also called single-cell organisms, are made of just one cell. They are thought to be some of the oldest forms of life, emerging up to 4 billion years ago. These tiny living things can be found in water, soil, and inside other organisms, and they survive in many different environments.

Scientists group unicellular organisms into two main categories: prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Prokaryotes, such as bacteria, do not have a nucleus and have simpler internal structures. Eukaryotes, such as single-celled algae and protozoa, have a nucleus and more complex parts inside the cell. Unicellular organisms matter because they produce oxygen and food, recycle nutrients, support food chains, and some can affect health by causing disease.
